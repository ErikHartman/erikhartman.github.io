APEX2

Data

The APEX2 dataset combines in-house assays across 11 bacterial strains with curated, strain-matched public measurements, giving 38,492 peptide–strain targets across 4,930 peptides. A broader activity dataset contains 49,188 peptides from public AMP records and sampled UniProt fragments. Accounting for overlap between these tasks, the collection contains 52,918 distinct sequences across the training, validation and test partitions.

Reformulation

APEX2 separates two questions: whether a peptide inhibits bacterial growth within the tested range, and at what concentration it does so. The model jointly predicts activity probability and MIC conditional on activity. Because serial dilutions constrain MICs to intervals, the potency loss penalizes predictions outside the experimentally supported interval rather than treating the reported concentration as an exact value.

Architecture and model generation

A shared sequence representation feeds three outputs: broad antimicrobial activity, strain-specific activity and conditional MIC. The encoders combine learned and physicochemical residue features with optional protein language model embeddings. Three-fold cross-validation evaluated 300 configurations spanning architectures and training settings. The selected 14-member ensemble combines CNNs, bidirectional LSTMs, bidirectional GRUs and a Transformer, with validation-based checkpoint selection.

Results

On the shared in-house test cohort of 195 peptides, APEX2 achieved a mean strain-level AUROC of 0.81 and AUPRC of 0.67. Its conditional MIC output achieved an interval concordance of 0.69. These were the highest values among the evaluated models for the corresponding outputs. APEX2 also achieved the highest interval concordance on the sequence-filtered external bacterial benchmark.

Architecture and training formulation

Comparisons on the full 369-peptide test cohort examined the contributions of architecture and training formulation. Both improved on the baseline; combining them gave the strongest performance. The hurdle formulation also reduced the systematic offset in predicted MICs and improved numerical prediction accuracy.

Recovery of human encrypted peptides

APEX2 recovered 11 of 15 known human encrypted antimicrobial peptides, compared with four for ApexPathogen. The peptides were absent as exact sequences from APEX2 training, although eight occurred within longer auxiliary-training sequences. Recovery required a score below the cutoff in at least one strain: MIC divided by activity probability below 128 for APEX2, or MIC below 128 µM for ApexPathogen.

ApexTox and ApexHemo

Data curation

Agent-assisted curation with human review consolidated measurements from 30 projects, recovering 21,408 cytotoxicity observations and 32,025 hemolysis observations across 1,774 distinct peptides. Questions about sequence identity, concentrations, normalization and response curves were resolved during review. Public quantitative assays and binary annotations supplemented the in-house data, retaining both measured endpoints and bounds from curves that did not cross 50% within the tested range.

Architecture

ApexTox and ApexHemo use the same general sequence-encoder framework as APEX2. Each ensemble member predicts a CC50 or HC50 and a fixed or learned Hill slope, defining a concentration–response curve. ApexTox additionally accounts for cell context. Training combines quantitative endpoints, censored bounds and measured responses at individual concentrations, while a separate broad classification head learns from binary toxicity evidence.

Results

ApexTox

ApexTox response curves generally shifted toward lower concentrations for peptides with lower measured CC50s. On the in-house test cohort of 145 peptides, its CC50 output achieved an AUROC of 0.78, compared with 0.60 for ToxinPred3, using experimental CC50 ≤50 µM to define positive labels.

ApexHemo

On the in-house test cohort of 118 peptides, ApexHemo’s HC50 output achieved an AUROC of 0.93, compared with 0.77 for the retrained HemoPI2 classifier and 0.70 for its HC50 regressor.
