01 The idea

Potency is only
part of the picture.

A promising antimicrobial peptide needs to inhibit bacteria at concentrations below those that damage host cells.

APEX2, ApexTox and ApexHemo bring these properties into the same discovery workflow. Together, they combine curated experimental data, modern neural architectures and training objectives that reflect how activity and toxicity are measured.

02 The approach

Learn from the experiment
as it was measured.

01

Curate the underlying evidence

Combine standardized in-house assays with public records. For toxicity, agent-assisted curation with human review brings scattered experimental measurements into a common format.

02

Separate activity from potency

APEX2 uses a two-part hurdle model: first the probability of activity, then the minimum inhibitory concentration conditional on activity. Broad AMP labels provide an additional learning signal.

03

Retain intervals and bounds

Serial dilutions place MICs within intervals. Toxicity curves may never cross 50% within the tested range. The training objectives preserve this information instead of assigning arbitrary endpoints.

04 From prediction to design

Search for the balance.

Optimizing antimicrobial potency alone can also increase predicted toxicity.

In the paper’s evolutionary search, including ApexTox and ApexHemo redirects optimization toward a more favorable predicted trade-off. The joint search favors higher CC50s and HC50s while maintaining improvement in antimicrobial fitness.

THE MANUSCRIPT

The next generation of APEX models for the discovery and design of safe antimicrobial peptides

Erik Hartman, Hanqun Cao, Marcelo D.T. Torres, Fangping Wang, Johan Malmström and Cesar de la Fuente-Nunez

Read manuscript draft ↗
Read the abstract

Antimicrobial peptides (AMPs) offer a promising avenue for the next generation of antibiotics, but identifying potent, selective and safe candidates is challenging. Predictive deep learning models have revolutionized the field and greatly accelerated the identification and design of potent AMPs, however, their performance remains modest. Here we present three new frontier models for antimicrobial activity, cytotoxicity and hemolysis prediction: APEX2, ApexTox and ApexHemo. These models combine high-quality curated in-house and public datasets, modern architectures and revised training formulations. APEX2 outperforms the evaluated models in activity discrimination and potency ranking, while ApexTox and ApexHemo outperform the evaluated public models in toxicity discrimination. Together, these models help prioritize peptides within a predicted therapeutic window, supporting efforts to discover the next generation of antibiotics.

APEX2, ApexTox and ApexHemo are brought together in ApexSuite, a common interface for peptide mining and model-guided design.
